# Goverly Adjacent Opportunity Map - 2026-05-21

## Decision Question

Where does a Goverly-like model have the best chance of working if adapted for the Ecosystem Intelligence concept?

Goverly's visible model is not just "procurement search." The useful pattern is:

1. Public scan or intake.
2. Readiness file.
3. Evidence and compliance gaps.
4. Fit/routing into buyers, primes, programs, or obligations.
5. Recurring monitoring plus services around transactions.

The best adjacent markets are therefore not generic databases. They need a policy or compliance trigger, fragmented suppliers, proof gaps, and buyers who need defensible routing.

## Bottom Line

The highest-potential adaptation is a **Canadian defence supplier readiness and routing product**, initially centered on cyber/security readiness and then expanded into Canadian-content, Arctic/Northern, and supply-chain fit.

The strongest first wedge is:

> **Defence supplier cyber, Controlled Goods, and contract-security readiness scan -> evidence gap file -> mission/opportunity fit -> referral or buyer-ready profile.**

This is a better fit than copying Goverly's ITB-only positioning because it has a clearer mandatory trigger, a broader supplier base, and stronger alignment with Ecosystem Intelligence's mission-to-engagement workflow.

## What Goverly Exposes As A Market Pattern

Goverly's visible product suggests several defensible product mechanics:

- A low-friction scan as acquisition.
- A buyer-readable file rather than a generic dashboard.
- Evidence gaps as the immediate "aha."
- Routing into known obligations, programs, or buyers.
- A mix of software, data, and advisory workflow.

The reverse-engineering effort is not mainly technical. A capable team could build the visible scan and readiness-report shell quickly. The hard part is the **data model, evidence library, buyer routing, and trust layer**.

## Opportunity Filters

I used these filters to rank opportunities:

- **Policy pull:** Is there a mandate, spend program, procurement preference, certification, or compliance deadline?
- **Supplier fragmentation:** Are suppliers spread across SMBs, regions, associations, directories, and informal networks?
- **Proof gap:** Do suppliers need to prove readiness, eligibility, certification, capacity, or Canadian/local/Indigenous content?
- **Buyer urgency:** Do primes, government teams, program managers, or economic development groups need defensible routing?
- **Competition gap:** Are current tools mostly directories, consultants, bid alerts, or broad marketplaces?
- **Execution fit:** Can the first version be built from public sources, structured intake, and human-reviewed enrichment?
- **Ecosystem Intelligence fit:** Does the niche support mission/use-case -> targets -> why now -> evidence -> Working List?

## 1. Defence Cyber, Controlled Goods, And Contract-Security Readiness

### Customer

- Canadian defence and dual-use SMBs.
- Firms entering DND, NATO, Five Eyes, or prime supply chains.
- Primes that need a cleaner view of supplier readiness.
- Cyber/compliance providers that need qualified demand.
- Regional economic development groups trying to prepare local firms.

### Pain

Suppliers do not just need to know that CPCSC, CMMC, Controlled Goods, or contract-security requirements exist. They need to know:

- Whether they are likely in scope.
- Which proof artifacts they already have.
- Which gaps block a bid, subcontract, prime conversation, or export opportunity.
- Which buyer/program/opportunity path is realistic now.
- Whether the organization should pursue Level 1 self-assessment, Level 2 readiness, Controlled Goods registration, organization security screening, or a lower-friction market first.

The official sources make this a timely problem:

- CPCSC Level 1 is available in April 2026 and enters select defence contracts in summer 2026.
- Level 2 is planned for select defence contracts beginning in spring 2027.
- The Controlled Goods Program page notes a surge in registration applications.
- Canada's Defence Industrial Strategy explicitly calls out the need to accelerate clearances, accreditation, SMB integration, supply-chain security, and industry navigation.

### Current Alternatives

- Cybersecurity consultants and MSPs.
- CMMC/CPCSC platforms such as [Kopit](https://www.kopit.ca/), which positions around CP-CSC and CMMC compliance.
- US-oriented CMMC tools and marketplaces such as IVIS and AIA's CMMC Marketplace.
- Generic spreadsheets, consultant checklists, internal prime questionnaires.
- Government guidance pages that explain requirements but do not route the supplier into a buyer-ready readiness file.

### Gap

The market has compliance tools, but there is room for a **defence opportunity readiness layer**:

> "What can this company credibly pursue, what proof is missing, and which buyer/program path should it take next?"

That is not the same as being a GRC platform. It is closer to a structured readiness brief plus routing workspace.

### Product Shape

- Free scan: cyber/security/export/readiness triage.
- Readiness file: CPCSC, CMMC, Controlled Goods, organization screening, data residency, sensitive information handling, prime-readiness evidence.
- Gap plan: which blockers must be closed before bid, subcontract, or buyer meeting.
- Buyer profile: a clean capability/readiness summary usable by primes or program teams.
- Partner marketplace: vetted cyber assessors, CGP/security-screening advisors, export-control counsel, proposal support.
- Recurring tracker: readiness status, evidence freshness, certification deadlines, opportunity fit.

### Business Model

- Free scan for acquisition.
- Paid readiness file: C$499-C$2,500 one-time depending depth.
- SMB subscription: C$199-C$799/month for evidence updates, monitoring, and profile maintenance.
- Partner referral or implementation revenue.
- Prime/economic-development workspace: C$10K-C$75K/year depending scale.

### Why This Is The Best First Niche

It has a mandatory trigger, recurring maintenance, visible supplier confusion, and an obvious workflow bridge into Ecosystem Intelligence's evidence-backed Working List. It also avoids directly copying Goverly's ITB lane.

### Risks

- Compliance claims must be precise; the product cannot imply certification unless certification has occurred.
- It may require a qualified cyber/industrial-security partner.
- Some compliance platforms may move upstream into readiness and routing.
- Overbuilding into GRC would create a harder product than needed.

### Experiment

Build a 10-question public scan and manually produce 10 readiness files for Canadian defence/dual-use SMBs. Sell the file before building the dashboard. Measure whether companies pay for a "buyer-ready defence readiness file" and whether primes or RDAs want aggregated views.

## 2. Canadian-Content / Buy Canadian Supplier Readiness

### Customer

- Canadian manufacturers and material suppliers.
- Federal contractors and construction/defence bidders.
- Primes needing domestic content evidence.
- Economic development and industry associations.

### Pain

The Buy Canadian policy changes the value of being visible, verifiable, and ready as a Canadian supplier. Suppliers need to prove more than existence:

- Canadian ownership or supplier status.
- Canadian-made content.
- Canadian materials.
- IP/R&D/value-added content.
- Capacity, certifications, regions served, lead times, and procurement readiness.

Buyers need to find credible Canadian suppliers without relying only on relationships, directories, and manual research.

### Current Alternatives

- SourceCan presents a Canada-first manufacturing marketplace with verification, supplier search, and RFQ workflows.
- General B2B marketplaces and supplier directories.
- Association lists.
- Internal procurement databases.
- Manual vendor outreach.

### Gap

Most supplier marketplaces optimize for discovery and RFQ. The gap is a **policy-aware Canadian-content readiness file** that can be attached to procurement, grant, defence, infrastructure, and prime-supplier conversations.

### Product Shape

- "Canadian Content Readiness Scan."
- Supplier profile with Canadian content evidence, certifications, materials, NAICS/commodity tags, regions, and fit to strategic procurement categories.
- Buyer workspace for sourcing Canadian suppliers in priority sectors.
- Evidence freshness and exception-risk flags.
- Optional RFQ workflow later.

### Business Model

- Supplier onboarding/readiness file.
- Sponsored or verified profiles, with caution to avoid pay-to-play credibility issues.
- Buyer subscription for verified supplier search.
- Enterprise/procurement team package for strategic sourcing.

### Best Narrow Wedge

Start with **defence construction, Arctic/NORAD infrastructure, shipbuilding sustainment, and critical materials**, not all Canadian manufacturing. This keeps it aligned with strategic procurement and avoids becoming a generic marketplace.

### Risks

- Broad marketplaces already exist or are emerging.
- Suppliers may resist paying unless buyer demand is visible.
- Verification must be credible and not just self-attested marketing.

## 3. Arctic/Northern Defence Infrastructure And Sustainment Readiness

### Customer

- Northern and Arctic suppliers.
- Indigenous and local businesses.
- Defence infrastructure contractors.
- Primes and integrators.
- Regional development agencies.
- Defence/NORAD-adjacent program teams.

### Pain

Arctic and Northern defence work needs more than technology matching. It needs local operating capacity, logistics, sustainment, infrastructure, Indigenous/local partnership, environmental constraints, and credible evidence that a supplier can perform in region.

Canada's Defence Industrial Strategy explicitly emphasizes the North and Arctic, domestic partners, supply-chain resilience, and infrastructure. RDII also supports regional businesses entering domestic and international defence supply chains.

### Current Alternatives

- Local knowledge and relationship networks.
- Economic development organizations.
- Association directories.
- Prime contractor vendor lists.
- Project-specific outreach.

### Gap

There is room for a **Northern supplier readiness and mission-fit file**, especially for infrastructure, sustainment, energy, telecoms, sensors, logistics, construction, and MRO.

### Product Shape

- Supplier scan focused on Arctic/Northern operating proof.
- Evidence file: region served, past performance, certifications, Indigenous/local status where applicable, logistics constraints, equipment, insurance, security posture, weather/remote operations, partnerships.
- Mission/use-case mapping: Arctic domain awareness, infrastructure resilience, remote sustainment, energy resilience, communications, mobility, emergency response.
- Buyer shortlist and gap view for primes or program teams.

### Business Model

- Paid readiness files for suppliers.
- Regional cluster maps for economic development partners.
- Prime/buyer subscription for Arctic/Northern capability discovery.
- Advisory packages for regional supplier development.

### Risks

- Relationship-heavy and trust-dependent.
- Smaller reachable market than generic procurement.
- Must avoid implying access to classified demand.
- Requires careful partnership posture with Indigenous and Northern organizations.

## 4. Dual-Use Accelerator / Challenge Adoption Routing

### Customer

- Dual-use accelerators.
- Defence innovation challenge programs.
- Test centres.
- Program sponsors.
- Cohort startups.
- Prime innovation teams.

### Pain

Programs produce cohorts and demos, but adoption often fails because buyers cannot quickly answer:

- Which mission problem does this solve?
- Is the company mature enough?
- What evidence supports the claim?
- What test, range, pilot, buyer, or prime path should come next?
- What is the gap between innovation-demo language and procurement/mission reality?

NATO DIANA, IDEaS, BOREALIS, RDII, and similar programs create an expanding pool of dual-use companies that need routing and evidence-backed briefings.

### Current Alternatives

- Pitch decks.
- Program CRM notes.
- Mentor reports.
- Manual portfolio reviews.
- Generic startup databases.

### Gap

There is room for a **post-cohort adoption readiness file** that turns startup claims into buyer-readable, evidence-backed mission fit.

### Product Shape

- Cohort readiness scan.
- Mission-fit matrix.
- Company/capability brief.
- Evidence gaps and next test recommendations.
- Buyer/primes briefing pack.
- Follow-on tracking after the accelerator.

### Business Model

- Program package: C$15K-C$75K per cohort depending size.
- Startup paid readiness upgrade.
- Sponsor workspace for portfolio tracking.
- Strategic briefings as service revenue.

### Risks

- Can become consulting-heavy.
- Budgets may be program-cycle dependent.
- Buyers may not trust startup self-reported evidence without independent validation.

## 5. Defence-Critical Minerals And Strategic Inputs Readiness

### Customer

- Critical mineral developers, processors, and component suppliers.
- Defence and industrial buyers.
- Investors.
- Economic development and trade teams.
- Primes needing supply-chain resilience.

### Pain

Canada's critical minerals strategy now explicitly emphasizes dual-use minerals in commercial and defence value chains. Buyers need to understand project readiness, provenance, processing capacity, offtake status, permitting, location, allied-market fit, and supply-chain risk.

### Current Alternatives

- Mining databases.
- Investor research.
- Consulting studies.
- Government lists.
- Manual supplier maps.

### Gap

The likely gap is not a mine database. It is a **defence-relevant supplier/project readiness layer** that can show which companies or projects are relevant to specific defence and strategic industrial needs.

### Product Shape

- Project/company readiness profile.
- Mineral/material mapping to defence/dual-use supply chains.
- Evidence and risk: permitting, ownership, processing, offtake, ESG/Indigenous partnership, allied-market status.
- Buyer/investor shortlist by mission or strategic-input need.

### Business Model

- Paid strategic-input market maps.
- Investor/buyer intelligence subscriptions.
- Supplier readiness files.
- Economic development packages.

### Risks

- Requires domain expertise.
- Sales cycles are slower.
- Data quality is harder.
- Could drift away from the current Ecosystem Intelligence wedge unless tightly scoped to defence supply-chain readiness.

## 6. Indigenous Procurement Readiness And Verification

### Customer

- Indigenous businesses.
- Procurement officials.
- Prime contractors.
- Economic development organizations.
- Service providers supporting Indigenous businesses.

### Pain

The mandatory 5% target creates strong demand for credible Indigenous supplier discovery, eligibility, readiness, and outreach. The official target page ties eligibility to the Indigenous Business Directory and modern treaty beneficiary business lists. The market also has integrity and trust concerns around procurement programs.

### Current Alternatives

- Indigenous Business Directory.
- Modern treaty lists.
- Procurement portals.
- Emerging platforms such as CONBEX.
- Consultants, associations, and community networks.

### Gap

The product gap is real, but this is not a good founder-led lane unless it is Indigenous-led or built through accountable partnerships. The right shape is an enabling product that supports Indigenous businesses and buyers without extracting value or becoming an untrusted gatekeeper.

### Product Shape

- Indigenous business readiness file.
- Eligibility/source evidence, capability, certifications, regions, capacity, and procurement-readiness gaps.
- Buyer outreach support with transparent consent.
- Community/partner governance.

### Business Model

- Partner-led platform or service.
- Procurement-readiness packages.
- Buyer/government/primes subscription.
- Service-provider marketplace.

### Risks

- Trust and governance risk.
- Eligibility sensitivity.
- Potential scrutiny around verification.
- Existing or emerging players may be better positioned.

## Lower-Priority Markets

### Generic Government-Contract Matching

This is crowded. GovWin, GovTribe, HigherGov, SamSearch-style tools, bid aggregators, and proposal AI products already address the obvious workflow. A new entrant needs a narrower proof/readiness wedge.

### Generic AI Governance Scanner

There is demand, but the category is broad and increasingly crowded. It is also less connected to Ecosystem Intelligence's defence and engagement-management wedge. A narrow version could work only if tied to defence procurement, model assurance, or critical-infrastructure vendor readiness.

### Health And Medtech Procurement

Healthcare procurement has real readiness and evidence gaps, but it is a slower, more regulated market with different buyer relationships. It is a watch-list niche, not a first adaptation.

## Gaps Across The Market

These gaps appear repeatedly across the strongest niches:

1. **Readiness is fragmented.** Suppliers need to understand certifications, security, evidence, eligibility, and buyer fit together, not as separate checklists.
2. **Directories do not explain "why this supplier, why now."** Search results are not enough for BD, prime, or public-sector decisions.
3. **Compliance tools stop too low.** They help complete controls but often do not connect readiness to mission fit, prime needs, government programs, or transaction paths.
4. **Programs produce cohorts, not adoption routes.** Innovation and funding programs often lack a durable post-program routing layer.
5. **Canadian-content and Indigenous procurement need evidence, not just labels.** Buyers need confidence that claims are backed by current records.
6. **Arctic/Northern capability is hard to inspect.** Regional operating proof, local partnership, logistics, and sustainment capability are not captured well in generic marketplaces.
7. **Most tools are supplier-side or buyer-side, not both.** The opportunity is to create a file that is valuable to the supplier and legible to buyers.

## Adaptation To Ecosystem Intelligence

The adaptation should not be "Goverly for everything." It should be a narrow workflow extension:

> Company/capability scan -> evidence-backed readiness file -> mission/use-case fit -> confidence and gaps -> saved Working List -> briefing-ready next step.

The most natural first module is:

### Defence Readiness File

Fields:

- Company identity and canonical sources.
- Capability summary.
- Relevant mission areas/use cases.
- Certifications and claimed standards.
- CPCSC/CMMC readiness status.
- Controlled Goods / contract-security indicators.
- Canadian-content indicators.
- Indigenous/local/Northern indicators where relevant and source-backed.
- Past performance and buyer evidence.
- Gaps and stale evidence.
- Suggested next step.
- Confidence level.
- Field citations.

This fits the existing project vocabulary: Company, Capability, Evidence, Derived Read, Working List, and Mission Area / Use Case.

## Product-Market Fit Hypotheses

### H1: SMBs will pay for a buyer-ready defence readiness file.

Evidence signal: CPCSC and Controlled Goods create real readiness anxiety. The test is whether firms pay for the file before software exists.

Test: Sell 10 manual readiness files to defence/dual-use SMBs.

Pass criteria: 5 paid files, 3 follow-on subscriptions or advisory referrals, and repeated buyer-facing use of the file.

### H2: Primes and RDAs need an aggregated supplier-readiness view.

Evidence signal: DIS and RDII emphasize SMB integration into defence supply chains.

Test: Offer a small regional or mission-area supplier readiness map to one RDA, association, or prime.

Pass criteria: paid pilot or written commitment for a curated supplier-readiness workspace.

### H3: Canadian-content readiness is valuable only when narrowed to strategic sectors.

Evidence signal: Buy Canadian applies to strategic federal procurement and materials-heavy construction/defence purchases.

Test: Build 25 Canadian-content supplier profiles in one narrow sector: defence construction materials, Arctic infrastructure, shipbuilding sustainment, or critical inputs.

Pass criteria: buyer pull from contractors/procurement teams, not just supplier interest.

### H4: Accelerator/challenge programs will pay for post-cohort mission routing.

Evidence signal: Dual-use programs create many companies that need adoption paths.

Test: Produce a mission-fit and evidence-gap matrix for one cohort.

Pass criteria: program sponsor pays for a second cohort or asks for an ongoing portfolio workspace.

## Recommended Next Move

Do not start by reverse-engineering Goverly's full product. Start with a **manual, service-led readiness file** in the highest-friction niche:

1. Pick one first wedge: **Canadian defence cyber/security readiness for SMBs**.
2. Build a one-page public scan intake.
3. Manually produce 10 readiness files.
4. Use the files to learn which fields actually matter to suppliers, primes, and advisors.
5. Only then implement a reusable scan/report workflow in Ecosystem Intelligence.

The first paid artifact should be named something like:

> Defence Supplier Readiness File

Avoid naming it a certification, score, or compliance guarantee.

## Strategic Recommendation

The best opportunity is not a better procurement search engine. It is a **readiness intelligence layer for strategically important suppliers**.

Most competitors either:

- help suppliers find opportunities,
- help buyers search directories,
- help companies manage compliance controls, or
- provide consulting.

The gap is the middle:

> Turn public evidence and supplier intake into a buyer-readable readiness file that explains what the company can credibly pursue, what proof is missing, and who should engage next.

For Ecosystem Intelligence, this is a strong fit if kept narrow:

- Maintain the mission-to-engagement wedge.
- Keep source-backed facts separate from derived reads.
- Use the Working List as the buyer-side action surface.
- Do not become a CRM, generic marketplace, or end-to-end compliance system.
