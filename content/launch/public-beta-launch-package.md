## Release position

True North Map is an independent, free public resource created and stewarded by Andrew Davies. It helps people find Canadian defence and dual-use organizations, understand what they build, connect technologies to public needs, inspect the evidence, and identify who may be worth speaking with next.

The release is a public beta, but it is not framed as an unfinished side project. It is a working Canadian discovery and connection resource with transparent gaps, human review, and clear participation paths.

## Founder catalyst and guiding story

Andrew Davies is a veteran and Combat Systems Engineering Officer whose experience spans military service and the civilian sector. As global events sharpened the importance of security, resilience, and sovereign capability, he felt a responsibility to focus his effort in Canada, in industries where that experience could be useful.

Work in defence-sector project delivery and engagement with operators, engineers, subject-matter experts, founders, and program teams revealed an ecosystem with a clear North Star: build more capability in Canada and answer the defence commitments this country has set for itself. Across the country, extraordinary technical talent, creativity, experience, and drive were coalescing into a genuine willingness to build and contribute.

The missing piece was a shared picture. Canada needed a practical way to see who was building, what their technology could do, where it might fit, what evidence supported it, and who should be brought into the next conversation.

True North Map is Andrew's contribution to that problem. It combines defence experience, combat-systems knowledge, public evidence, and ecosystem relationships to help companies, organizations, and people find one another and contribute faster.

The North Star:

> Map what Canada can build. Connect the people ready to build it. Help the whole ecosystem move together.

The larger belief is simple: no single company, program, region, or person can meet the scale of the challenge alone. Shared visibility makes collaboration easier. Rising tides lift all boats, and Canada can accomplish ambitious goals when the people ready to contribute can find one another.

I built True North Map after repeatedly seeing capable Canadian defence and dual-use organizations disappear inside fragmented regional, program, and industry networks.

The public beta is free to explore. It maps more than 90 reviewed organizations and more than 80 technologies or offerings, with source-linked profiles, public needs, PDF and CSV exports, private Working Lists, reviewed contributions, and a human-brokered connection workflow.

Ask True North is a constrained discovery assistant over the published corpus. It can select only known record and citation IDs, the server rejects unknown entities, and deterministic search takes over if the model is unavailable or cannot produce a valid answer.

The research workflow is also deliberately review-first. Agents can stage evidence-backed candidates but cannot publish. Unknown data remains unknown, and technology-to-need fit is labelled as an assessment rather than endorsement or procurement eligibility.
